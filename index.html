Task Formulas & High-Yield Hacks

Essay (200-300 words, ~20 min)

🏆WIN CONDITION: Simple, 100% correct sentences beat ambitious broken ones. Spelling + grammar are heavily weighted.
1 · Introduction
The increasing influence of <topic> in our daily lives has led to various discussions and debates. This issue is crucial because of its influence on <impact area>, affecting both individuals and society. In this essay, I will examine <topic> by considering (advantages and disadvantages / causes and effects), and provide my perspective on the matter.
2 · Body Paragraph 1
One of the main (advantages / causes) related to <topic> is <point 1>. Studies indicate that <topic> (promotes / leads to) <outcome>. Moreover, another significant (advantage / cause) is <point 2>, which strongly affects people's lives. For instance, <specific example>.
3 · Body Paragraph 2
On the other hand, there are also (disadvantages / consequences) associated with <topic>. A major (drawback / effect) is <point 3>, which becomes more obvious in situations such as <example>. Furthermore, <point 4> (creates obstacles / causes further impact), influencing both individuals and society.
4 · Conclusion
In conclusion, <topic> has (both advantages and disadvantages / significant causes and effects) that must be considered carefully. Looking ahead, it is essential to focus on maximizing the benefits while reducing the drawbacks. Consequently, this approach will ensure better outcomes for the future.

Summarize Written Text (SWT)

🏆WIN CONDITION: ONE sentence only — no full stop in the middle. Watch subject-verb agreement. Keep under 75 words.
[Main idea], and [support 1], while [support 2], which [result/conclusion].

Alternative safe formula:
(Noun 1) can be a major aspect that relates to modern society these days, so people must pay close attention to it; therefore, the importance of (Noun 2) must not be overlooked because (Noun 3) may affect (Noun 4), and the text explains that (Noun 5) and (Noun 6) are very important.

Summarize Spoken Text (SST)

🏆WIN CONDITION: Scored on Content 4 / Form 2 (50-70 words) / Grammar 2 / Vocab 2 / Spelling 2. Hit exactly 50-70 words and spell-check!
The lecture discussed [topic]. The speaker first explained [point 1]. Then, [point 2]. Additionally, [point 3]. In conclusion, [final point/implication].

Describe Image (DI) (~40 sec)

🏆WIN CONDITION: Keep talking — fluency scores even if you skip a detail. NEVER pause > 3 seconds.
This [graph/chart/image] shows [topic/title].
The key feature is [highest/lowest/biggest trend].
Additionally, [second feature/color].
It is also notable that [third feature/number].
In conclusion, [overall trend/implication].

Re-tell Lecture (RL)

Take 3-4 keyword notes while listening; speak fluently from them.
The lecture was about [topic].
The speaker discussed [point 1], [point 2] and [point 3].
They emphasized [key detail].
In conclusion, [main message].

📚 Reading: Fill in the Blanks Hacks

You don't always need to know the vocabulary. Use Grammar Hacks to eliminate options.
1. Preposition Clues: Look at the word immediately AFTER the blank.
• blank + to (e.g., contribute to, lead to, dedicated to)
• blank + of (e.g., capable of, aware of)
• blank + for (e.g., responsible for)
2. The "To + Verb" Rule: If the blank is directly after the word "to", 90% of the time you need a base verb (V1) with no -ing or -ed. (e.g., to improve, to discover).
3. The "A/An" Rule: If the blank follows "a", the word must start with a consonant sound. If it follows "an", it must start with a vowel sound. This easily eliminates 50% of dropdown options.
4. Parallelism (And/Or): If two words are joined by "and", they usually match in form.
• "She was running and [blank]." -> The blank must be an -ing word (e.g., jumping).

👂 Listening: WFD & RS Hacks

WFD: The Initials Method (Highest Yield)
Listen to the whole sentence first. As you hear it, type ONLY the first letter of every word.
Audio: "The library is located on the north side of the campus."
You type: T l i l o t n s o t c
Then, use those initials to reconstruct the sentence immediately.
WFD: The Grammar Check
Most points are lost on Plurals (-s), Past Tense (-ed), and Articles (a/an/the). If you aren't sure if the speaker said "student" or "students", type both! (e.g. "The student students..."). The computer only counts correct words and ignores extras.
RS: The "Meaning Chunk" Strategy
Do NOT try to memorize word-by-word. Group them.
"Please come to the next seminar // properly prepared."
If you forget the ending, say "Please come to the next seminar" with perfect fluency. Fluency + Pronunciation score on partial content. Silence scores zero.

🚨 Exam-Day Checklist

🎙️
Warm up your voice with 5 Read Alouds before you leave home.
🎧
Arrive early and test the microphone properly at the start. Ensure it's positioned correctly (beside your mouth, not directly in front of your breath).
🗣️
Speak at a consistent volume. Don't trail off or whisper at the end of sentences.
⏱️
Manage Writing timing. Don't overspend time on the Essay and leave yourself 2 minutes for Summarize Written Text (SWT).
⌨️
In WFD, keep typing! Even if you only caught 4 words out of 10, type them. Partial words still score points.
⏩
Don't dwell on a hard item. If an MCQ is too hard, guess and move on. Fluency and momentum matter more than any single low-yield answer.
