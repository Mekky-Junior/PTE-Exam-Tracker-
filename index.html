Core Strategy

PTE uses integrated (cross) scoring — one spoken or typed answer earns marks in two skills at once. Your two highest targets (Speaking 88 and Writing 85) are driven by tasks that also feed Reading and Listening. Over-invest in a handful of cross-scored "super-tasks" and the lower targets rise almost for free.

Cheat-Sheet, Formulas & Strategies

Memorize these formulas. In the exam, grammar, spelling, fluency and "never stop talking" win the marks.

Essay (200-300 words, ~20 min)

WIN CONDITION: Simple, 100% correct sentences beat ambitious broken ones. Spelling + grammar are heavily weighted. Connectors: Firstly / Moreover / For instance / However / In contrast / Consequently / In conclusion.
1 · Intro (Paraphrase + Thesis + 2 Points)
The increasing influence of <topic> in our daily lives has led to various discussions and debates. This issue is crucial because of its influence on <impact area>, affecting both individuals and society. In this essay, I will examine <topic> by considering (advantages and disadvantages / causes and effects), and provide my perspective on the matter.
2 · Body Paragraph 1 (Topic + Explain + Example)
One of the main (advantages / causes) related to <topic> is <point 1>. Studies indicate that <topic> (promotes / leads to) <outcome>. Moreover, another significant (advantage / cause) is <point 2>, which strongly affects people's lives.
3 · Body Paragraph 2 (Topic + Explain + Example)
On the other hand, there are also (disadvantages / consequences) associated with <topic>. A major (drawback / effect) is <point 3>, which becomes more obvious in situations such as <example>. Furthermore, <point 4> (creates obstacles / causes further impact), influencing both individuals and society.
4 · Conclusion (Restate + Summarize + Recommend)
In conclusion, <topic> has (both advantages and disadvantages / significant causes and effects) that must be considered carefully. Looking ahead, it is essential to focus on maximizing the benefits while reducing the drawbacks.

Summarize Written Text (ONE sentence, 5-75 words)

WIN CONDITION: ONE sentence only — no full stop in the middle. Watch subject-verb agreement and keep it under 75 words.
"[Main idea], and [support 1], while [support 2], which [result/conclusion]."
Alternative (Noun Connector)
(Noun 1) can be a major aspect that relates to modern society these days, so people must pay close attention to it; therefore, the importance of (Noun 2) must not be overlooked because (Noun 3) may affect (Noun 4), and the text explains that (Noun 5) and (Noun 6) are very important.

Summarize Spoken Text (50-70 words)

WIN CONDITION: Scored on Content 4 / Form 2 (MUST be 50-70 words) / Grammar 2 / Vocab 2 / Spelling 2. Hit the word count and spell-check!
The lecture discussed [topic]. The speaker first explained [point 1]. Then, [point 2]. Additionally, [point 3]. In conclusion, [final point/implication].

Describe Image (~40 sec)

WIN CONDITION: Keep talking — fluency scores even if you skip a detail. Never pause >3 seconds.
1. This [graph/chart/image] shows [topic].
2. The key feature is [highest/lowest/biggest trend].
3. Additionally, [second feature].
4. It is also notable that [third feature / colors].
5. In conclusion, [overall trend/implication].

Re-tell Lecture

WIN CONDITION: Take 3-4 keyword notes while listening: speak fluently from them. Fluency > perfect content.
1. The lecture was about [topic].
2. The speaker discussed [point 1], [point 2] and [point 3].
3. They emphasized [key detail].
4. In conclusion, [main message].

📚 Reading FIB Grammar Hacks

Grammar rules can solve 50% of the blanks without even knowing the vocabulary meaning.

1. Parts of Speech

  • Article + [Blank] + Noun: The blank is an Adjective. (e.g., an important decision)
  • Preposition + [Blank]: Usually a Noun or V-ing (Gerund). (e.g., by implementing)
  • To + [Blank]: Almost always Verb in base form (V1). (e.g., to analyze)

2. Collocations & Prepositions

  • Look at the word immediately after the blank.
  • Words pair naturally: associated with, responsible for, contribute to, focus on.
  • Memorize academic collocations (e.g., draw a conclusion, make an impact).

3. Subject-Verb Agreement

  • Singular subject = Verb with 's' (e.g., The study shows)
  • Plural subject = Base verb (e.g., The researchers show)
  • Look for tricky subjects like "A number of" (plural) vs "The number of" (singular).

4. Context Clues (Conjunctions)

  • And / Or: Words on both sides must be the same type (Positive AND Positive, Noun AND Noun).
  • But / However / Although: Look for a contrasting opposite word.

🔄 Re-Order Paragraphs Hacks

  • Find the Independent Sentence: This is the topic sentence. It stands alone. It will NEVER start with pronouns (He, They, It) or linking words (However, Moreover, Therefore).
  • Look for Pairs (Nouns to Pronouns): If Sentence A says "Thomas Edison", Sentence B saying "He" comes after it.
  • Time Sequences: Look for dates (1990 comes before 2005) or sequence words (Firstly, Initially -> Eventually, Finally).
  • A/An vs The: "A company" (introducing it) always comes before "The company" (referring back to it).

✍️ WFD: The "Initials" Method

This is the single most powerful technique for Write from Dictation. It guarantees you capture the sequence of words.
  • Step 1 (Listen): Do not start typing immediately. Listen to the whole sentence first.
  • Step 2 (Type Initials): As the speaker talks, rapidly type the FIRST LETTER of every word on your notepad (or screen).
    Audio: "The assignment is due on Thursday." -> You type: "T a i d o T"
  • Step 3 (Reconstruct): Immediately after the audio stops, expand the initials into full words while the audio is still in your short-term memory.
  • Step 4 (Grammar & Spell Check): This is crucial. Check for 's' / 'es' (plural nouns, singular verbs), 'ed' (past tense), and articles (a, an, the) — that is where most points are lost.
  • Hack: If you are unsure between two words (e.g., "student" vs "students"), write BOTH. "The student students is..." You might get points for the correct one without penalty (check latest Pearson scoring rules, but adding extra valid words often works).

📝 Note-Taking (SST & RL)

  • Never try to write full sentences. You will fall behind.
  • Write down Nouns, Verbs, and Adjectives. Ignore "the, a, is, on".
  • Use symbols (&, ->, =, X).
  • If you miss a point, drop it immediately and listen for the next one. Don't freeze.

🖱️ HIW & FIB (Listening)

  • HIW: Keep your mouse cursor directly under the words as the speaker talks. Do not look away from the screen.
  • FIB: Do NOT use the mouse to click to the next box. Use the TAB key on the keyboard. Type fast, and correct spelling errors in the 10-second check at the end.

🗣️ The "Golden Rule" of Speaking

FLUENCY BEATS ACCURACY. The computer scores your rhythm, pacing, and lack of hesitation MUCH higher than your pronunciation or content perfection.

🎤 Microphone & Breathing

  • Position the mic exactly between your nose and upper lip, about 2 fingers away. This avoids picking up heavy breathing (plosive sounds like P and B).
  • Take a deep, comfortable breath before the beep.
  • Speak at a moderate, consistent volume. Do not whisper, do not shout.

🚫 The "Never Correct" Rule

  • If you stumble on a word, mispronounce it, or skip it entirely... DO NOT go back to fix it.
  • Self-correction destroys your Oral Fluency score (which drags down Reading/Listening too).
  • Just keep moving forward smoothly like nothing happened.

📖 Read Aloud Phrasing

  • Use prep time to chunk into phrases and mark stressed words.
  • Read in "meaningful chunks" (3-5 words at a time).
  • Do not read word-by-word like a robot. Connect words smoothly.
  • If you encounter a word you don't know, mumble it confidently or substitute it instantly. Do not freeze.

🧠 Repeat Sentence Strategy

  • Capture 3-4 word meaning-chunks, not single words.
  • Repeat IMMEDIATELY with the same rhythm and intonation.
  • If you only catch 50% of the sentence, say that 50% with absolute confidence and perfect fluency. Silence scores zero.

📋 Exam-Day Checklist

  • Warm up your voice with 5 Read Alouds before you leave home.
  • Arrive early; test the microphone properly at the start (don't rush this!).
  • Speak at a consistent volume; don't trail off or whisper at sentence ends.
  • Manage Writing timing — don't overspend on the essay and starve your SWT time.
  • In WFD, keep typing every word you caught; partial words still score marks.
  • Don't dwell on a hard item — fluency and momentum matter more than any single answer. Keep moving forward.
